\section{Analysis plan and deviations}
\label{sec:s_protocol}
\textbf{Analysis plan.}
The estimators and their configurations, the reference simulator V0, the leave-one-route-out split and the metrics were fixed in a written analysis plan before any test-route evaluation, so the comparison of V0-trained with real-trained estimators is confirmatory.
The simulator variants, the calibration and equal-budget designs, the fixed-road speed analysis, the spectral comparisons, the band-limited retraining, joint training for all variants and the pass-density analysis were specified in written plans before they were run, but after the test routes had been evaluated; six further analyses were added after some of their results were known (the equal-budget comparison for V1--V8, the per-draw comparison of the calibrated variants with real-only training, the speed scaling of raw vibration in simulation, the paired elasticity differences, the real-only vibration index as a second comparator and the ranking by untrained vibration statistics).
All of them are exploratory: every variant, draw and $k$ is reported, and none of them was used to select a configuration.
The plans were not registered publicly.
Two hypotheses were stated before the corresponding runs.
That joint training beats real-only training on average for every variant up to 10\,km and stays within 0.02\,m/km of it at 20\,km \poHoneMeas{} for V0--V8 and \poHoneAll{} with V10.
That retraining on the features without vibration above 10\,Hz at least halves the spread of the sensor variants' levels while dropping the 10--15\,Hz band alone changes it by less than 20\,\% \blHthreeHolds, and V4 stays outside their range (\blHthreeBHolds).
Several analyses had already been computed independently on the same data before the plans were written (joint training for V0, V1, V3 and V5, the band-limited retraining, the paired ranking test, the speed-robustness rows and the no-simulation index), so neither they nor the two hypotheses are confirmatory; where they overlap, our runs reproduce those values (joint training for every fold and draw, and the band-limited retraining, exactly).
The game-engine elevation diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:s_game} was added during the preparation of this manuscript.

\textbf{Deviations from the plans.}
\begin{itemize}
\item V7 and V8 use a random half of their passes (seed 7) to match V1 and V2 in size; the full sets are in Table~\ref{tab:variants_s_gbm}.
\item A planned aliasing check within one telematics unit was infeasible (TMU\#10 logged at 52.6\,Hz only on M13 and at 333\,Hz only on CPH1 and M3; TMU\#12 on M3 had no common segment $\times$ speed cell); it was replaced by an all-unit comparison on common cells, which is confounded with device and mounting.
\item The keys (segment, task, pass) of the real units are not unique (12 duplicates where a ring-road pass covers a segment twice); predictions are aligned by position and checked against the labels.
\item Units shorter than one Welch window have no spectrum and are excluded from the spectral cell means.
\item The sensor-robustness experiment was first run with independent draws per perturbation level and then re-run with common random numbers (variance reduction); the first run is kept in \path{results/sim/robustness_v1_independent_draws.csv}.
\item Zero-shot CNNs (three seeds, two simulation sets) and CNNs calibrated with paired drives were limited for compute before their results were known.
\item Neural-net metrics are those of the five-seed ensemble prediction; per-seed means are in \path{results/v2/summary_real_estimators.csv}.
\item The telematics configuration of V0 (55--62\,Hz, 0.01\,g, tilt s.d.\ 6$^\circ$) was set from a preliminary inspection of the unlabelled LiRA-CD files. A complete re-measurement gives nominal rates of \liraRateLow\,Hz (\liraRateLowShare\,\% of passes) or \liraRateHigh\,Hz, 0.01\,g quantisation in \liraLsbShare\,\% of passes and tilts with median \liraTiltMedian$^\circ$ (maximum \liraTiltMax$^\circ$). V0 was kept; the matched configurations are the variants V5 and V6.
\item The no-simulation comparison also reports the vibration index fitted to V0 simulated units with a level offset (Table~\ref{tab:variants_s_index}); the band associations were also computed against the motorway-minus-urban level difference. Neither is used for any claim.
\end{itemize}

\section{Labels and the game-engine benchmark}
\label{sec:s_game}
\textbf{IRI labels.}
On the \iriValFiles{} LiRA-CD route-directions whose profile and IRI files are consistent, our IRI implementation reproduces the IRI reported by the P79 with $r=$\,\iriValR, bias \iriValBias\,m/km and MAE \iriValMae\,m/km over \iriValN{} segments of 100\,m (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_iri}a; an algorithm check, since both are computed from the same laser profiles).
For the eighth file (CPH6, direction HH) the reported IRI cannot be aligned with the file's own profile (best $r=$\,\alignBestR{} over shifts of $\pm$3\,km, with or without reversal, against $r=$\,\alignControlR{} at zero shift for two control files); all labels are therefore recomputed from the profiles.
A second profilometer (ARAN9000, surveyed on other dates) is compared in Fig.~\ref{fig:s_iri}c and Table~\ref{tab:s_aran}.

\textbf{Game-engine corpus.}
The corpus contains \geTrips{} manually driven trips (\geKm\,km) of \bngCars{} BeamNG.tech vehicles on \bngMaps{} maps.
The inertial signals, the position and the ray-cast elevation of the road mesh under each wheel path were polled by separate threads: \bngImuDupMin--\bngImuDupMax\,\% of consecutive inertial rows are exact sample-and-hold duplicates, and \geRepeatMin--\geRepeatMax\,\% of the elevation readings repeat the previous reading while the car moves (\geRepeatMean\,\% on average, mostly in runs of at most ten readings).
\ifmainten{Fig.~\ref{M-fig:profile_psd} of the main paper}{Fig.~\ref{fig:profile_psd}} compares the displacement spectra of the logged elevation with those of the measured P79 profiles: the game-engine elevation carries \gePsdUrbanLong{} times the power of the urban P79 profiles at wavelengths of 10--30\,m and \gePsdUrbanShortMin--\gePsdUrbanShortMax{} times at 0.5--5\,m (\gePsdMotorwayLong{} and \gePsdMotorwayShortMin--\gePsdMotorwayShortMax{} times that of the motorway profiles).
The IRI of its non-overlapping 100\,m segments averages \geIriLogged\,m/km from the logged elevation and \geIriFresh\,m/km when the repeated readings are removed before interpolation, so the excess lies in the elevation values, not in their sampling.
A curvature-adaptive smoothing designed to remove mesh tessellation lowers the mean to \geIriSmoothed\,m/km, removing \bngCorrReduction\,\% of the summed IRI and changing the condition class of \bngClassChange\,\% of the segments.
Applied to the measured P79 profiles, which contain no mesh facets, the same smoothing lowers the IRI of urban segments by \legacyCorrUrbanMape\,\% on average (bias \legacyCorrUrbanBias\,m/km) and that of motorway segments by \legacyCorrMotorwayMape\,\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_iri}b), so neither the smoothed nor the unsmoothed labels are a valid IRI ground truth.

\textbf{Transfer and generalisation.}
Trained on the corpus and evaluated on LiRA-CD as in the main paper, LightGBM reaches a within-route MAE of \vvBcorrMaeWithin\,m/km with the smoothed and \vvBrawMaeWithin\,m/km with the unsmoothed labels (within-route $\rho$ \vvBcorrRhoWithin{} and \vvBrawRhoWithin; Table~\ref{tab:variants_s_gbm}), and its estimates rise steeply with speed on a fixed road (elasticity \vvBcorrElastPooled{} and \vvBrawElastPooled).
The vibration index trained on the same corpus ranks segments within routes far better ($\rho$ \vvBcorrIdxRhoWithin{} with the smoothed labels) but misses the level (MAE \vvBcorrIdxMae\,m/km, bias \vvBcorrIdxBias\,m/km; Table~\ref{tab:variants_s_index}), the pattern of ranking without level seen with the parametric simulator.
Within the corpus, LightGBM trained on two maps reaches an MAE of \bngSoneCorrGbmMae\,m/km on the third, against \bngSoneCorrConstMae\,m/km for the training median, and with both vehicle and map unseen no estimator is substantially better than the training median (MAE \bngStwoConstMae; vibration index \bngStwoPhysMae; LightGBM \bngStwoGbmMae\,m/km, unsmoothed labels).

\section{Simulator}
\label{sec:s_sim}
The half-car is discretised exactly under a first-order hold and solved in modal coordinates; the solution deviates by at most \simModalDev{} (relative) from a direct state-space recursion and by \simRkDev{} from a fine-step Runge--Kutta integration.
The sampled vehicles have body modes at \simBodyHzLo--\simBodyHzHi\,Hz and wheel-hop modes at \simHopHzLo--\simHopHzHi\,Hz.
Speed is drawn independently of roughness (base speed 20--110\,km/h with Ornstein--Uhlenbeck fluctuations and random braking events).
Table~\ref{tab:simranges} lists the parameter ranges; the variants and their seeds are defined in \path{experiments/sim_generate.py}.
Besides the variants of the main paper, V10 adds the synthetic profiles of V9 to the measured ones.

Speed alone ranks segments only weakly ($\rho=$\,\vrSpeedRhoWithin).

\section{Spectra and speed: additional results}
\label{sec:s_spectra}
Table~\ref{tab:s_spectra} gives the band means of the spectral differences in Fig.~\ref{M-fig:spectra}a, Table~\ref{tab:s_gap} the 10--15\,Hz ratio by road class and the speed scaling of raw vibration in simulation and reality, Table~\ref{tab:s_band_assoc} the association of every band's amplitude ratio with the zero-shot bias and level ($R^2$ \baRtwoHighBias{} for 10--15\,Hz and \baRtwoRmsBias{} for the overall RMS), and Table~\ref{tab:s_band_limited} the band-limited retraining.
The 10--15\,Hz deficit is larger on motorway than on urban segments for \bgcMotorwayLarger{} of \bgcNvar{} variants, as is the level error.
Passes logged at 333\,Hz contain more, not less, 10--15\,Hz power than passes logged at 52.6\,Hz on the same cells (52.6 minus 333\,Hz: \spAliasHigh{} in log$_{10}$, confounded with the device), so the excess is not an aliasing artefact; in the 333\,Hz passes, \hfTenTwenty\,\% of the vibration energy above 0.3\,Hz lies at 10--20\,Hz, \hfAboveTwenty\,\% above 20\,Hz and \hfAboveNyq\,\% above the 26.3\,Hz Nyquist frequency of the slower logging (medians over \hfUnits{} units).

\textbf{Speed on a fixed road.}
Each segment is driven about ten times at different speeds (median within-segment SD of log speed \elSdLogv), so the speed dependence of an estimate can be measured on a road of constant roughness: the within-segment elasticity is the slope of the log estimate on log speed with segment fixed effects, and $\pm$10\,\% between 40 and 100\,km/h corresponds to $|$elasticity$|\le$0.104 (Fig.~\ref{fig:speed}, Table~\ref{tab:s_speed}; 1\,km-block intervals for single elasticities).
The real car's vibration RMS rises by \elRawPooledPct\,\% between 40 and 100\,km/h (elasticity \elRawPooled{} [\elRawPooledLo, \elRawPooledHi]).
The estimators trained on real cars over-correct for it: LightGBM's estimate changes by \elRealGbmPooledPct\,\% over the same range (\elRealGbmPooled{} [\elRealGbmPooledLo, \elRealGbmPooledHi]), also without speed inputs (\elRealGbmNoSpeedPooled), the MLP and CNN behave alike (\elRealMlpPooled{} and \elRealCnnPooled), and the vibration index over-corrects most (\elRealIdxPooled).
V0-trained LightGBM changes by \elSimGbmPooledPct\,\% (\elSimGbmPooled{} [\elSimGbmPooledLo, \elSimGbmPooledHi]); paired on the same passes, its elasticity exceeds that of the real-trained model by \epVzeroPooled{} [\epVzeroPooledLo, \epVzeroPooledHi] (5\,km blocks), entirely on urban roads (\epVzeroUrban{} [\epVzeroUrbanLo, \epVzeroUrbanHi]; motorways \epVzeroMotorway{} [\epVzeroMotorwayLo, \epVzeroMotorwayHi]), and across the nine variants the paired difference ranges from \epMeasPooledMin{} to \epMeasPooledMax{} (interval excluding zero for \epMeasPooledExcl{} of 9).
These elasticities change little with telematics-unit fixed effects or with the within-pass speed SD as a covariate (Table~\ref{tab:s_speed_rob}), except that slow urban passes are more dynamic (within-segment correlation of log speed and log speed SD \srCorrLsdUrban): with the speed SD as a covariate the real-trained urban elasticity shrinks to \srRealUrbanLsd{} [\srRealUrbanLsdLo, \srRealUrbanLsdHi], while the paired urban difference stays at \srDiffUrbanLsd{} [\srDiffUrbanLsdLo, \srDiffUrbanLsdHi].
The speed coefficient of variation, tied to $1/v$ by construction (correlation \srCorrCvUrban{} with log speed), absorbs most of the speed variation within urban segments and turns that difference negative (\srDiffUrbanCv{} [\srDiffUrbanCvLo, \srDiffUrbanCvHi]); on urban roads speed and speed variability cannot be fully separated, so the smaller urban over-correction of simulation-trained LightGBM is not established.

\section{Controlled simulation: other vehicles and sensors}
\label{sec:s_controlled}
LiRA-CD contains one car model, so transfer to other vehicles was examined in simulation with freshly simulated test data (Table~\ref{tab:general}, Fig.~\ref{fig:vehicles}).
With the amount of training data fixed at 1,200 passes, the MAE on 30 unseen vehicles exceeds that on the training vehicles by \vsThreeRel\,\% with 3 training vehicles, \vsThirtyRel\,\% with 30 (the number used in V0) and \vsHundredRel\,\% with 100.
The upper 95\,\% bound of this increase is at most 10\,\% from 100 vehicles on with a segment bootstrap but not with a route bootstrap (upper bounds \vsHundredRelHiRoute{} and \vsThreeHundredRelHiRoute\,\% for 100 and 300 vehicles).
The gap closes from both sides: the error on unseen vehicles falls (\vsThreeUnseen{} to \vsHundredUnseen\,m/km from 3 to 100 vehicles) and the error on training vehicles rises as each gets fewer passes (\vsThreeSeen{} to \vsHundredSeen\,m/km).
At $N=30$, a vehicle's error is only weakly related to its parameter distance from the nearest training vehicle (Spearman \vsDistRho{} over 30 vehicles).
Sensor perturbations at test time (Table~\ref{tab:general}, right; Fig.~\ref{fig:s_robust}) reproduce in simulation what the variants showed on the real car: the sensor path moves the level.
A holder resonance at 15\,Hz turns a bias of \rbvGbmBiasMountRigid{} into \rbvGbmBiasMountFifteen\,m/km; 25\,Hz sampling and a speed error of $+$20\,\% are the most harmful perturbations (MAE \rbvGbmMaeRateTwentyFive{} and \rbvGbmMaeSpeedPlus\,m/km), white noise, tilt and lost samples the least.
